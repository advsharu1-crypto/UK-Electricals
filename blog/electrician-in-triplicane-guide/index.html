How to Choose a Reliable Electrician in Triplicane, Chennai

Finding an electrician is easy. Finding one you are happy to leave alone with your wiring is harder. This guide gives you a plain way to judge any electrician in Triplicane before work starts.

  • By UK Electricals
  • Published 7 November 2026
  • 5 min read
The short answer

A reliable electrician looks at the problem before quoting, explains it in plain words, tells you what the price includes and does not hurry you. Ask what the job covers, which materials will be used and what happens if the fault returns. Be careful with anyone who gives a firm figure without seeing the problem or asks for a large payment up front.

Start with the job, not the name

Before you phone anyone, decide what kind of job you have. The questions to ask are different for each one.

  • A fault. A socket is dead, a breaker keeps tripping, lights flicker. Nobody can price this honestly without looking at it first.
  • A new installation. A fan point, extra sockets, a new light layout. This can usually be scoped from a short description or a few photos.
  • Renovation wiring. Walls are open and old wiring is exposed. This needs a plan, and it needs to be agreed before the plastering is redone.

Write down when the problem happens and what you see. "The kitchen socket sparks when the mixer is plugged in" is far more useful to an electrician than "electrical problem".

What a careful electrician does before quoting

Looks at the problem

For faults, a quote given over the phone is a guess. A careful electrician will say that an inspection comes first, then give you a figure for the actual work.

Explains in plain words

You should come away knowing what is wrong, what will be done and why. If the explanation is all jargon or all reassurance, ask again.

Puts the scope in writing

A WhatsApp message is enough. It should say what work is included, what is not, who supplies the materials and how payment is staged. This protects both sides.

Questions worth asking

  • Will you inspect the fault before giving an estimate?
  • What does the price include: labour, materials, making good the wall?
  • Which cables, switches and breakers will you use? Do they carry a BIS or ISI mark?
  • Will you isolate the circuit before you start work on it?
  • Will you test the circuit when you finish and show me the result?
  • If the same fault comes back, what happens?
  • How and when do you expect to be paid?

You do not need to be technical to ask these. How the electrician answers tells you a lot.

Licences and paperwork

In Tamil Nadu, installation work done by contractors falls under the state's electrical licensing rules, and what is required depends on the type and size of the work. Ask the electrician which licence or permit applies to your job and what it covers. If you want to be sure, confirm with the state electrical licensing authority.

This website does not publish licence or certificate details for UK Electricals. If that matters for your job, ask us directly, as you would ask anyone else.

Red flags

  • A firm price for a fault nobody has seen.
  • Pressure to decide today, or a claim that everything must be replaced.
  • A request for most of the money before any work starts.
  • Work on a live circuit when the supply could easily be switched off.
  • No answer when you ask which materials will be used.
  • Reluctance to put anything in writing.

Things that are specific to Triplicane

Many homes here sit in older buildings with narrow staircases, shared meters and wiring that has been added to over the years. That changes the conversation.

  • Old wiring. Ask the electrician to look at what is already there, including the earthing, before adding new load to it.
  • Access. Tell them about stairs, lanes and parking. It affects how long the job takes.
  • Building rules. In an apartment, check with your association before changes to shared wiring or the meter room.
  • Timing. Agree working hours, especially if neighbours or tenants are affected.

Comparing two or three estimates

A low total can simply mean something has been left out. Put the estimates side by side and check the same things in each: what work is listed, whether materials are included, what quality of fittings is named and what is excluded.

If one estimate is far lower, ask what is different. There may be a good answer. If there is not, you have learned something useful.

After the work is done

  • Keep the bill and the message that set out the scope.
  • Ask for the circuits to be labelled at the distribution board.
  • Take a photo of any wiring that will be hidden behind a wall or ceiling.
  • Note the date, so you can say when the work was done if a fault returns.

If you would like to talk through a job, see our residential electrical services or contact us.

Key takeaways

  • Decide whether the job is a fault, a new installation or a renovation. Each is scoped differently.
  • Ask for the scope, the materials and the payment terms in a message you can keep.
  • Compare what each estimate includes, not only the total.
  • Ask which licence or permit applies to your job. Do not accept a vague yes.
  • Tell the electrician about building rules, access and timings before the visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should an electrician inspect the fault before giving a price?

For faults, usually yes. The cause of a tripping breaker or a dead socket cannot be known from a phone description. For a clearly defined installation, such as adding a fan point, a price from a description or photos can be reasonable.

Is it risky to hire someone recommended by a neighbour?

Not necessarily. A recommendation is a good start. Still ask the same questions about scope, materials and payment, because a neighbour's job may have been different from yours.

How do I know if my job needs a licensed contractor?

It depends on the type and size of the work. Ask the electrician which licence or permit applies, and confirm with the state electrical licensing authority if you are unsure.

What should I do before the electrician arrives?

Clear access to the distribution board and the affected area, note when the fault happens, take a photo of the board and tell your building association if shared wiring may be involved.
